    What these numbers mean

    TermDefinitionIn plain terms
    Daily move% change from yesterday's close to today's latest close.How much the price moved since yesterday.
    Weekly move% change over the last 5 trading days (~1 calendar week).How much the price moved over roughly the last week.
    6-month move% change over roughly the last 126 trading days.How the stock has done over the last half-year — good for spotting the bigger trend beyond daily noise.
    YTD move% change since the first trading day of this calendar year.How the stock has done since January 1st this year.
    CUSUM regime testA real change-point statistic. It flags "upside_shift" or "downside_shift" only when recent returns have persistently drifted away from the stock's own baseline — not just one noisy day.A math test that checks "has this stock's behavior actually, durably changed lately, or was that just one weird day?" It only sounds an alarm for real, sustained shifts.
    Bayesian regime posteriorP(bull) / P(neutral) / P(bear) computed via Bayes' rule from recent daily returns. Hypothesis means/spread are calibrated to each stock's own historical baseline (not a shared generic assumption), starting from an equal 1/3 prior.It's asking "given how this stock has actually traded the last couple weeks, is that more consistent with a bullish stretch, a bearish stretch, or just normal noise for this stock?" and giving you a percentage confidence for each, updated fresh every time you load the page.
    Setup score0–100 blend: 25% momentum, 20% trend, 15% drawdown position (all simple heuristics), 40% Bayesian bull posterior (real stats) — then volatility and CUSUM are applied as adjustments, not weighted factors. The BUY/WAIT/AVOID cutoffs (78/58) are fixed thresholds, not backtested against actual forward returns — treat the score as a structured opinion, not a validated prediction.A single 0-100 "how good does this setup look right now" number. Higher is more favorable, but it's a structured opinion built from the factors above — not a proven predictor of what happens next.
    Volatility control (factor row)Not one of the four weighted score factors. It shows this stock's 20-day daily-return stdev and the direct penalty subtracted from the score afterward (−3 points per 1% stdev, capped at −30). A "low" score here just means the penalty is large, i.e. the stock has been choppy recently — it does not mean volatility was weighted like momentum/trend/drawdown/regime were.How "jumpy" the stock has been lately. Choppier stocks get points taken off the score as a caution flag, on top of everything else.
    Recent catalyst (1M)The trailing 1-month move, shown for context only. It is NOT part of the composite score calculation — it's informational, so you can see if a big recent move is driving your read of the setup even though the score itself doesn't count it.Just shows you the last month's move for context — it doesn't affect the score, it's there so a big recent pop or drop isn't a surprise when you look at the rest of the numbers.
    Trailing 28 trading daysThe last ~6 weeks of trading, split into five rolling 5-trading-day "Week" blocks. These are relative windows within the trailing period, not calendar weeks of the current month.A quick look at the last ~6 weeks, broken into 5 chunks, so you can see whether the stock has been trending up, down, or bouncing around recently.
    1-month expected range (±1σ)A statistical projection over the next 21 trading days, using this stock's own historical daily-return mean and volatility (random-walk approximation: mean scales by horizon, spread scales by √horizon). Roughly a 68% confidence interval — not derived from the setup score, and not a guarantee.A rough guess at where the price could land in about a month, based on how this stock has actually behaved historically. Think of it as "probably somewhere in this range," not a promise.
    Risk labelLow/Moderate/High, based purely on the 20-day volatility reading (thresholds: ≥3.2% = High, ≥1.8% = Moderate, else Low). Independent of the score.A simple gut-check on how bumpy the ride might be, separate from whether the setup looks good.
    Confidence labelLow/Medium/High, based on how decisive the Bayesian posterior is (top probability) combined with whether CUSUM also flagged a shift. High conviction means both signals agree; Low means neither is decisive.How sure the tool is about its own read. High means the different signals agree with each other; Low means they're mixed or unclear.
